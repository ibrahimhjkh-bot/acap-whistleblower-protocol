Overcoming the Whistleblower's Dilemma via Zero-Knowledge Threshold Escrow Architecture
A Position Paper on Collective Action Protocols for Corporate Oversight
Anonymous Collective Action Protocol (ACAP) Working Group
Open Security & Policy Research Series • October 2026
Abstract
Institutional whistleblowing suffers from a systemic collective action failure. Isolated insiders face severe asymmetric retaliation risks when reporting corporate wrongdoing, while traditional reporting mechanisms force a trade-off between dangerous early public exposure and easily suppressed internal channels. This paper proposes the Anonymous Collective Action Protocol (ACAP), an architectural framework leveraging Zero-Knowledge proofs (ZKPs), client-side metadata sanitization, and threshold escrow logic to align insider incentives. By withholding payload disclosures until a verified quorum (N ≥ Threshold) is reached, ACAP neutralizes the first-mover disadvantage and guarantees safety in numbers.

1. Introduction & Problem Statement

Corporate wrongdoing relies fundamentally on information asymmetry and fear of retaliation. While modern legal frameworks—such as the EU Whistleblower Directive (EU 2019/1937) and the US Sarbanes-Oxley Act—theoretically protect informants, empirical evidence indicates that lone whistleblowers face catastrophic career, financial, and personal consequences (Alford, 2001).

Existing channels force a flawed binary: internal compliance portals often lead to immediate employer monitoring, whereas open public leaks void statutory protections and expose individuals to defamation litigation. To resolve this, we present ACAP: a cryptographic escrow system designed to convert isolated individual disclosures into coordinated, threshold-verified collective action.

2. Game-Theoretic Foundation

Whistleblowing can be modeled as an N-player Prisoner's Dilemma. Let i ∈ {1, &dots;, N} represent insiders possessing evidence of systemic fraud. If player i acts alone (N = 1), the corporate entity concentrates 100% of its retaliatory resources on player i. The payoff matrix heavily disincentivizes early disclosure.

System State Individual Action Collective Quorum Expected Individual Payoff
Uncoordinated (Traditional) Solo Leak (N = 1) Unmet Severe Retaliation / High Penalty
Escrow Phase (ACAP S0) Encrypted Deposit N < T Zero Risk / Zero Exposure
Activation (ACAP S1) Automatic Dispatch N ≥ T Protected Regulatory Disclosure

ACAP reshapes this payoff structure by introducing a threshold escrow function F(D). Submissions Di remain encrypted and unreleased in state S0 while N < T (where T is the safety quorum). When N ≥ T, state transitions to S1, releasing the consolidated dossier simultaneously to regulators and press networks. This eliminates the "first-mover penalty," ensuring no single participant stands exposed.

3. Technical & Governance Architecture

To ensure credibility without compromising anonymity, ACAP integrates three core technical guarantees:

[ User Client ] ---> (1. ZK-DKIM Proof) ---> [ Domain Verification ] | +-----------> (2. WASM Sanitize) ---> [ Stripped Metadata ] | +-----------> (3. Threshold Escrow) -> [ State S0: N < T ] | (Quorum Reached: N >= T) | v [ State S1: Public / Regulatory Release ]

A. Anonymous Credential Verification: To prevent competitor Sybil attacks without harvesting user identities, ACAP employs Zero-Knowledge DKIM (ZK-DKIM) circuit verification. Users prove ownership of a cryptographically signed corporate email header from target domain dtarget without revealing their specific mailbox address (Kang et al., 2023).

B. Client-Side Sanitization: Before payload transmission, local WebAssembly modules strip EXIF tags, author metadata, and steganographic watermarks, neutralizing corporate "canary file" tracking strategies.

C. Responsible Regulatory Routing: Unlike unmoderated public forums, threshold activation routes dossiers directly to accredited enforcement agencies (e.g., SEC, national labor inspectorates) and investigative press consortia, preserving the reporter's legal immunity under international law.

4. Policy Implications & Legal Alignment

By replacing public board posting with structured, threshold-gated regulatory dispatch, ACAP avoids host-side defamation liability while strengthening public interest oversight. Regulators receive pre-validated, high-signal evidentiary bundles representing multiple independent sources, significantly reducing investigative overhead while shielding vulnerable insiders.
