  <div class="abstract-box">

    <div class="abstract-title">Abstract</div>

    Institutional whistleblowing suffers from a systemic collective action failure. Isolated insiders face severe asymmetric retaliation risks when reporting corporate wrongdoing, while traditional reporting mechanisms force a trade-off between dangerous early public exposure and easily suppressed internal channels. This paper proposes the <strong>Anonymous Collective Action Protocol (ACAP)</strong>, an architectural framework leveraging Zero-Knowledge proofs (ZKPs), client-side metadata sanitization, and threshold escrow logic to align insider incentives. By withholding payload disclosures until a verified quorum (<i>N</i> &ge; <i>Threshold</i>) is reached, ACAP neutralizes the first-mover disadvantage and guarantees safety in numbers.

  </div>


  <section>

    <h2>1. Introduction & Problem Statement</h2>

    <p class="no-indent">Corporate wrongdoing relies fundamentally on information asymmetry and fear of retaliation. While modern legal frameworks—such as the EU Whistleblower Directive <a class="cite" href="#ref-eu2019">(EU 2019/1937)</a> and the US Sarbanes-Oxley Act—theoretically protect informants, empirical evidence indicates that lone whistleblowers face catastrophic career, financial, and personal consequences <a class="cite" href="#ref-alford2001">(Alford, 2001)</a>.</p>

    <p>Existing channels force a flawed binary: internal compliance portals often lead to immediate employer monitoring, whereas open public leaks void statutory protections and expose individuals to defamation litigation. To resolve this, we present ACAP: a cryptographic escrow system designed to convert isolated individual disclosures into coordinated, threshold-verified collective action.</p>

  </section>


  <section>

    <h2>2. Game-Theoretic Foundation</h2>

    <p class="no-indent">Whistleblowing can be modeled as an <i>N</i>-player Prisoner's Dilemma. Let <i>i</i> &in; {1, &dots;, <i>N</i>} represent insiders possessing evidence of systemic fraud. If player <i>i</i> acts alone (<i>N</i> = 1), the corporate entity concentrates 100% of its retaliatory resources on player <i>i</i>. The payoff matrix heavily disincentivizes early disclosure.</p>


    <table class="matrix-table">

      <thead>

        <tr>

          <th>System State</th>

          <th>Individual Action</th>

          <th>Collective Quorum</th>

          <th>Expected Individual Payoff</th>

        </tr>

      </thead>

      <tbody>

        <tr>

          <td>Uncoordinated (Traditional)</td>

          <td>Solo Leak (<i>N</i> = 1)</td>

          <td>Unmet</td>

          <td>Severe Retaliation / High Penalty</td>

        </tr>

        <tr>

          <td>Escrow Phase (ACAP <i>S</i><sub>0</sub>)</td>

          <td>Encrypted Deposit</td>

          <td><i>N</i> &lt; <i>T</i></td>

          <td>Zero Risk / Zero Exposure</td>

        </tr>

        <tr>

          <td>Activation (ACAP <i>S</i><sub>1</sub>)</td>

          <td>Automatic Dispatch</td>

          <td><i>N</i> &ge; <i>T</i></td>

          <td>Protected Regulatory Disclosure</td>

        </tr>

      </tbody>

    </table>


    <p>ACAP reshapes this payoff structure by introducing a <i>threshold escrow function</i> <i>F</i>(<i>D</i>). Submissions <i>D<sub>i</sub></i> remain encrypted and unreleased in state <i>S</i><sub>0</sub> while <i>N</i> &lt; <i>T</i> (where <i>T</i> is the safety quorum). When <i>N</i> &ge; <i>T</i>, state transitions to <i>S</i><sub>1</sub>, releasing the consolidated dossier simultaneously to regulators and press networks. This eliminates the "first-mover penalty," ensuring no single participant stands exposed.</p>

  </section>

  <section>

    <h2>3. Technical & Governance Architecture</h2>

    <p class="no-indent">To ensure credibility without compromising anonymity, ACAP integrates three core technical guarantees:</p>


    <div class="diagram-box">

[ User Client ] ---> (1. ZK-DKIM Proof) ---> [ Domain Verification ]

       |

       +-----------> (2. WASM Sanitize) ---> [ Stripped Metadata ]

       |

       +-----------> (3. Threshold Escrow) -> [ State S0: N < T ]

                                                   |

                                            (Quorum Reached: N >= T)

                                                   |

                                                   v

                                             [ State S1: Public / Regulatory Release ]

    </div>


    <p class="no-indent" style="margin-top: 6px;"><strong>A. Anonymous Credential Verification:</strong> To prevent competitor Sybil attacks without harvesting user identities, ACAP employs Zero-Knowledge DKIM (ZK-DKIM) circuit verification. Users prove ownership of a cryptographically signed corporate email header from target domain <i>d</i><sub>target</sub> without revealing their specific mailbox address <a class="cite" href="#ref-zkdkim2023">(Kang et al., 2023)</a>.</p>

    <p class="no-indent" style="margin-top: 6px;"><strong>B. Client-Side Sanitization:</strong> Before payload transmission, local WebAssembly modules strip EXIF tags, author metadata, and steganographic watermarks, neutralizing corporate "canary file" tracking strategies.</p>

    <p class="no-indent" style="margin-top: 6px;"><strong>C. Responsible Regulatory Routing:</strong> Unlike unmoderated public forums, threshold activation routes dossiers directly to accredited enforcement agencies (e.g., SEC, national labor inspectorates) and investigative press consortia, preserving the reporter's legal immunity under international law.</p>

  </section>


  <section>

    <h2>4. Policy Implications & Legal Alignment</h2>

    <p class="no-indent">By replacing public board posting with structured, threshold-gated regulatory dispatch, ACAP avoids host-side defamation liability while strengthening public interest oversight. Regulators receive pre-validated, high-signal evidentiary bundles representing multiple independent sources, significantly reducing investigative overhead while shielding vulnerable insiders.</p>

  </section>
